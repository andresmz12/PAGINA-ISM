Deductible business expenses: what counts and what does not

Every well-classified expense lowers what you pay in taxes. We explain the most common categories, the limits and how to back them up.

ISM Consulting TeamNovember 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • A deductible expense must be ordinary and necessary for your business.
  • Personal expenses are not deductible, even if paid with the business card.
  • Without receipts, a deduction can be lost in a review.
  • Classifying expenses well during the year saves time and money at filing.

Paying tax on your profit, not on everything you collect, is one of the advantages of owning a business. But it only works if your expenses are well recorded and truly belong to the business.

The basic rule

For the IRS, an expense is deductible when it is ordinary (common in your type of business) and necessary (helpful and appropriate to run it). It does not have to be indispensable, but it must have a clear connection to your activity.

Common categories

  • Advertising and marketing: ads, cards, website, social media.
  • Business materials and supplies.
  • Software and subscriptions you use to work.
  • Professional services: accountant, attorney, design.
  • Business insurance and licenses or permits.
  • Bank and payment processor fees.
  • Payments to contractors and employees.
  • Vehicle: miles driven for work (with a log) or actual expenses, depending on the case.
  • Home office: only if you use part of your home regularly and exclusively for the business.
  • Phone and internet, in the proportion used for the business.
  • Equipment and tools, which may be deducted at once or over several years.

What usually causes problems

  • Mixing personal and business expenses in the same account.
  • Meals: in general only part is deductible, and it must have a business purpose.
  • Deducting expenses without a receipt or a record of what they were for.
  • Deducting 100% of something used only partly for the business.

How to back up your expenses

Keep the receipt or invoice and note the date, amount and purpose. The IRS recommends keeping records for at least three years from when you filed, and longer in some cases. Taking photos of receipts with your phone is enough if they are legible and organized.

Why classification matters

An expense in the wrong category can lose its deduction or draw the IRS’s attention. That is why at ISM we organize every expense using the Schedule C categories, the form where they are reported.

Deduction rules have limits and exceptions that depend on each case. Ask before taking a significant deduction.
