How to choose a tax preparer and avoid scams

The signs of a trustworthy professional, the questions to ask and the practices to avoid at all costs.

ISM Consulting TeamNovember 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • A paid preparer must have a PTIN and sign your return.
  • Be wary of anyone who charges a percentage of your refund or promises a bigger refund.
  • Never sign a blank or incomplete return.
  • Review the return before signing it and ask for a copy.

Choosing who you trust with your taxes and personal data is an important decision. Honest preparers are the majority, but there are practices you should know about to protect yourself.

Signs of a good preparer

  • Has a PTIN and writes it on your return, as the IRS requires.
  • Signs the return they prepare.
  • Asks for documents and asks questions before calculating anything.
  • Explains what is in your return in terms you understand.
  • Gives you a copy and is available if the IRS writes to you later.
  • Charges a clear fee, before starting.

Red flags

  • Charges a percentage of your refund.
  • Promises a large or guaranteed refund before reviewing your documents.
  • Asks you to sign a blank or unreviewed return.
  • Does not sign the return or give their PTIN.
  • Deposits your refund into an account that is not yours.
  • Advises you to include income, expenses or dependents that are not real.

Useful questions before hiring

  • What credentials do you have (CPA, EA, attorney, or other)?
  • How much do you charge and what is included?
  • Will you help me if the IRS writes to me or audits me?
  • How will you receive and protect my documents and data?
  • How long does it take and how do I reach you?

A note on immigration matters

In the U.S., a “notario público” is not an attorney. For immigration matters, always consult an attorney or an accredited representative. At ISM we focus on taxes, accounting, ITIN and businesses.

Remember the responsibility is yours

Even if someone else prepares your return, you are responsible for what it says. That is why you should understand what you sign and keep your copy.
