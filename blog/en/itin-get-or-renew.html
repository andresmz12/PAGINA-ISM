ITIN: everything you need to know to get or renew your number

The ITIN is essential for filing taxes, opening bank accounts, and building your credit history in the U.S. We explain the complete process.

ISM Consulting TeamJanuary 2025 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • The ITIN is an IRS tax ID number for people who do not qualify for an SSN.
  • It is requested with Form W-7 and documents proving your identity.
  • It expires if not used on a return for 3 consecutive years.
  • A certified acceptance agent can verify your original documents so you do not have to mail them.

The ITIN (Individual Taxpayer Identification Number) is a tax ID issued by the IRS for people who do not qualify for a Social Security Number (SSN). If you are an immigrant or foreign worker in the U.S., the ITIN is your gateway to the American tax system.

Who needs an ITIN?

  • People without work authorization but with a tax filing obligation
  • Spouses or dependents of U.S. citizens or permanent residents
  • Foreign nationals with U.S.-sourced income subject to withholding
  • Students, teachers, or researchers on F, J, M, or Q visas

Required documents

To apply for an ITIN, you need to complete IRS Form W-7 along with documentation proving your identity and foreign status. Accepted documents include a valid passport, U.S. visa, birth certificate with official photo ID, foreign driver's license, among others.

Application process

The process can take 7 to 11 weeks if you apply directly to the IRS by mail. However, working with a Certified Acceptance Agent (CAA) like ISM Consulting Services makes the process faster and safer — you don't need to mail your original documents to the IRS.
